VR Basics Using Unreal Engine

13 jan 20252025 年 1 月 13 日 · 2 min read约 2 分钟读完

这篇文章写于在学校的课程期间，原文是英文。

Overview

In class, I had a series of introductory learning about the basics of virtual reality (VR) through Unreal Engine (UE) and delved deeply into the blueprint system in UE. Unreal Engine’s powerful graphics processing capabilities and blueprint system provide us with a very intuitive game development environment. Especially in the VR field, its ease of use and powerful functions make the creation of virtual reality experiences more convenient and efficient.

We first learned the basic operations of VR devices, particularly how to connect VR head-mounted devices with Unreal Engine. We used Oculus Meta Quest2 devices, which can be seamlessly connected to Unreal Engine.

When starting to create virtual reality projects, we chose a basic VR template. The VR templates provided by Unreal include necessary input controls and interaction settings, allowing us to focus on the construction of the virtual world. We designed a simple virtual scene through virtual environment construction tools (such as the Landscape tool) and added VR cameras and handheld controllers. Through these settings, participants can move freely, observe, and interact in the virtual world.

We learned how to achieve basic user interactions in VR, including hand movements, grabbing objects, and button triggering. In Unreal, VR interactions are realized through blueprints. The blueprint system enables the creation of complex interaction functions without writing a large amount of code. For example, we bound the key inputs of VR controllers through blueprints, so that users can use the controllers in their hands to click, grab, and touch objects in the scene. In this way, users can interact with the virtual environment in a more natural way, enhancing the immersion.

Blueprints are the visual scripting system of Unreal Engine, allowing developers to design game logic by dragging and dropping nodes instead of writing traditional code. Blueprints, through the combination of events, functions, and variables, enable us to easily control the behaviour and interaction of games. During the learning of blueprints, I mastered the basic node operations, such as creating variables, setting functions, and binding events.

Conclusion

Through this learning of the VR basics and the blueprint system of Unreal Engine, I have a more comprehensive understanding of virtual reality development. Unreal’s powerful VR support and visual programming tool (blueprints) enable me to quickly build interactive experiences and implement complex game logic.
