Week 1 Reading

13 apr 20252025 年 4 月 13 日 · 3 min read约 3 分钟读完

这篇文章写于在学校的课程期间，原文是英文。

This week, I have read three very interesting but also quite mind-bending articles. They all talked about how we interact in the digital world, view images, and use technology. These theories are closely related to what I’m studying in creative computing. Although they are quite abstract when reading, I will use some examples to explain what I have understood.


Ricmedia. (n.d.). TCP/IP protocol suite diagram [Online image]. Available at: https://www.ricmedia.com/tutorials/tcp-ip-protocols-a-short-guide (Accessed: 5 February 2025)

The first article is “Protocol vs Protocol” written by Galloway. He talks about the concept of “protocol”, but it’s not the social etiquette we usually understand. Instead, it refers to the rules governing the operation of the network, such as how data is transmitted between computers. Galloway argues that even though the Internet appears to be free and open, there are actually many invisible control mechanisms behind it. For example, fundamental communication protocols like TCP/IP determine whether information can be transmitted, to whom it is sent, and how it is transmitted. This reminds me that when we create digital works, we are also working in an environment restricted by these “rules”. For instance, when designing a website or a VR experience, the structure has already been pre-set by these systems, and we are merely creating within the existing framework.


PBC Today. (2018). 2D to 3D Models: Entering the Third Dimension [Online image]. Available at: https://www.pbctoday.co.uk/news/digital-construction-news/bim-news/2d-to-3d-models-entering-the-third-dimension/40555/ (Accessed: 5 February 2025)

The second article is “Technical Images” by Flusser. He talks about something called “technical images”, which are not the images seen with our naked eyes, but rather those generated by devices such as cameras, computers, and VR headsets. He emphasizes that much of the “reality” we see nowadays is actually a “reconstructed version” processed by these devices. For example, mobile phone maps, AR filters, or 3D modeled buildings. These images seem very realistic, but in fact, they are all the results of a bunch of codes and programs. This makes me realize that it’s possible that we are not “seeing the world”, but rather “seeing the world shaped by technology”. This perspective has had a great impact on me because I used to think that digital images were just “tools”, but now I’m starting to regard them as a powerful medium that can influence the way we think.


KnowItAll Ninja. (n.d.). New Media [Online image]. Available at: https://www.knowitallninja.com/dashboard/lessons/new-media/ (Accessed: 5 February 2025)

The third article is “What is New Media?” by Manovich. He summarizes “new media” with several key words, such as digitization, programmability, modularity, and so on. What he means is that new media is not simply the “electronic version of old things”, but rather a whole new cultural system. For example, Instagram is not just a digital version of an album. It has its own interactive logic, algorithmic ranking, and filter aesthetics, all of which influence how we create and communicate. I think this is very important for creative computing because what we are doing is not just “moving art onto the computer”, but rather reinventing the means of expression using languages like code, data, and interaction.

Overall, these three articles have made me more clearly aware that creative computing is not just about programming and then creating art, but rather about understanding the logic, structure, and impact behind the technology. Whether it’s VR, web pages, or apps, on the surface, it seems to be about interaction design, but at the underlying level, it actually involves a lot of “invisible” rules, programs, and cultural concepts.

Reading list

  1. Galloway, A. (2004). Protocol: How Control Exists After Decentralization, Chapter 2: Protocol vs Protocol
  2. Flusser, V. (2011). Into the Universe of Technical Images, Chapter 3: Technical Images
  3. Manovich, L. (2001). The Language of New Media, Chapter 1: What is New Media?
