Artificial creativity and its Impact

13 jan 20252025 年 1 月 13 日 · 3 min read约 3 分钟读完

这篇文章写于在学校的课程期间，原文是英文。

Overview

After reading the paper “Artificial Creativity: Emergent Notions of Creativity in Artificial Societies of Curious Agents” by Rob Saunders and John S. Gero (referred to as talkSaunders for short), I have gained an in – depth understanding of the complex nature of creativity.

The paper mentions that creativity is not only a manifestation of individual ability or talent, but also the result of interaction with the sociocultural environment. The systems view proposed by Csikszentmihalyi emphasises that creativity stems from the dynamic interaction between the creator and the surrounding environment, rather than an isolated individual process. For instance, the themes an artist chooses to create may be influenced by cultural norms, social expectations, and even other artists. This perspective makes me realise that creativity is essentially a symbiotic relationship, which needs to be understood within a specific context.

By reading the theories in the paper and combining them with my records in “quote for robot”, I’ve learned that artificial creativity systems aim to simulate the creative process rather than simply imitate individual creative thinking. These systems analyse the interactions among agents and the evaluation process of works through novelty detectors and hedonic functions, thus exploring how creativity emerges in collaboration and competition. The paper cites Martindale’s “novelty law”, demonstrating how society’s demand for innovation influences the evolution of style. This approach provides experimental tools for studying the formation of creativity in the sociocultural context.

The experiments in the paper reveal that agents in the artificial creativity system form cliques based on their preferences for novelty. This group phenomenon is also common in the real – world creative fields. For example, some styles may only be popular within specific artistic circles and have difficulty breaking through to enter the mainstream. This makes me reflect on how to avoid such internalised limitations in creative practice to ensure that creations can reach a broader audience.

The paper emphasises that artificial creativity systems typically operate in a closed environment, where agents evaluate works solely based on internal evaluation criteria. This closed – nature may limit their connection with external demands, yet it also provides a pure experimental environment, facilitating the study of the development of creativity under specific conditions. This reminds me that creative expressions in the real world need to go beyond the evaluations within the group and incorporate diverse perspectives and social expectations to enhance their applicability and influence.

Through reading talkSaunders and writing quote for robot, I’ve gained a deeper understanding of the multi – layered nature of creativity. In particular, the perspective on how the sociocultural environment profoundly influences creative expression has been extremely beneficial to me.

This has made me re – evaluate how to strike a balance between individual expression and group dynamics in my creative computing and interactive art practices. For instance, in my interactive installation projects, the feedback from participants is a crucial element, which is similar to the agent interactions in artificial creativity systems. However, over – reliance on the feedback from a specific group may limit the diversity of my creations. This inspires me to introduce more extensive user testing in future creations, expanding the influence of participants from diverse backgrounds and perspectives on my works.

Moreover, the “laboratory” characteristic of artificial creativity systems makes me think about how to create a similar controlled environment in my research to test the impact of different social situations on creativity. This approach might help me explore the innovative mechanisms in game design and narratology more systematically.
