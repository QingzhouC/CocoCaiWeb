Final Reflection

22 may 20262026 年 5 月 22 日 · 3 min read约 3 分钟读完

这篇文章写于在学校的课程期间，原文是英文。

The development of the TeleAgriculture project allowed me to explore the relationship between environmental data, interaction design, and spatial visualisation within a collaborative interdisciplinary workflow. Throughout the project, I primarily focused on designing and implementing the globe-based visualisation system, as well as contributing to the overall UI direction and interaction architecture of the platform.

One of the most valuable aspects of this project was learning how to translate abstract environmental datasets into an interactive visual experience. During the early stages, much of the information collected from the existing TeleAgriculture platform felt fragmented and difficult to interpret. Through research and iterative prototyping, I became increasingly interested in how geographic interfaces and layered interaction systems could make complex environmental information more understandable and engaging for users.

The globe visualisation became the core outcome of this exploration. Rather than presenting data through static charts alone, I wanted users to experience environmental information spatially, moving from a global perspective into country-level and city-level analysis. The addition of the time slider also became an important design decision, as it allowed the platform to connect Omar’s archived environmental datasets with Vidula’s future prediction models. This helped transform the interface from a simple monitoring tool into a speculative environmental forecasting system.

Working collaboratively with Nana was also an important part of the process. While I focused on the globe interaction structure and visual navigation flow, Nana expanded the system into detailed dashboard interfaces, analytical views, and onboarding pages. Passing the globe interaction framework to her and seeing it evolve into a more complete data ecosystem helped me better understand how collaborative design workflows operate in larger digital systems.

The user testing stage was particularly useful in revealing how different users interpreted the prototype. Many participants responded positively to the navigation structure, colour palette, and globe interaction, especially the way data was visualised geographically. However, the feedback also highlighted areas that could be improved, including reducing interface complexity, simplifying information density, and introducing clearer onboarding and customisation systems.

One important insight from this testing process was that users often evaluated the prototype as if it were a fully developed commercial application rather than a speculative interaction prototype. This revealed that the visual polish and interaction fidelity successfully communicated a sense of realism, but it also created expectations for features that were outside the scope of our contribution. Reflecting on this, I realised the importance of clearly framing the goals and limitations of a prototype during user evaluation sessions.

Technically, this project also helped me gain more confidence in React-based workflows and interactive web development. Implementing the globe system using react-globe.gl, Three.js, and React Router allowed me to better understand how frontend structures, real-time interaction, and visual rendering systems work together within a larger application architecture.

Overall, the TeleAgriculture project strengthened my understanding of:

  • spatial interaction design
  • environmental data visualisation
  • collaborative UI/UX workflows
  • frontend prototyping in React
  • speculative ecological interface design

Most importantly, the project demonstrated how digital interfaces can be used not only to display environmental data, but also to create new ways of thinking about ecological systems, prediction, and global interconnectedness.
