Reflections on Prometheus 2.0: Frankenstein Conquers the World

13 jan 20252025 年 1 月 13 日

这篇文章写于在学校的课程期间，原文是英文。

Marc Garrett’s article “Prometheus 2.0: Frankenstein Conquers the World!” deeply explores the intersection of mythology, technology, and human ethics. As a student majoring in creative computing, this article has made me rethink the profound significance of technological progress and its impact on society.

Garrett defines technology as a product of human evolution, originating from the Greek word “tekhne”, which means art and craftsmanship. This view reminds me of the learning process in creative computing, where innovation often stems from the integration of human creativity and technical skills. However, the article also reveals the exploitative tendency of humans in technological development. People keep altering raw materials and ecosystems while ignoring the long – term impacts. This coincides with the ethical considerations I frequently face in my projects: how to innovate with technology while avoiding over – consumption of resources or damage to the environment.

Through Mary Shelley’s Frankenstein and the myth of Prometheus, the article reveals the dual nature of creation, which is both a gift and a burden. Dr. Frankenstein attempted to surpass human limits, but his creation ultimately exposed the moral and social issues brought about by unrestrained ambition. As Garrett emphasises, the Promethean scientist symbolises a state of co – existing potential and danger. This theme prompts me to reflect on the motivations behind my technological designs and the possible consequences.

Reading “Prometheus 2.0” has made me keenly aware that technology requires a balance between its potential and ethics. I’ve learned that we should not only pursue innovation but also take into account the social and environmental impacts.
