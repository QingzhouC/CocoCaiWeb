What Interactivity Should Be

18 nov 20242024 年 11 月 18 日 · 4 min read约 4 分钟读完

这篇文章写于在学校的课程期间，原文是英文。

Overview

Interactivity lies at the core of how we interact with the world, whether through digital platforms, physical devices, or hybrid experiences. For interactivity to truly have an impact, it must follow some core principles. The following are the characteristics that I think interactivity should possess.

Characteristics

Good interactivity strikes a balance between providing freedom and maintaining structure. If users are given too much freedom, they may feel confused or lost; conversely, an overly rigid structure may seem restrictive and deprive users of the sense of autonomy that interactivity brings (Gaver, 1991). This balance is crucial in ensuring a meaningful user experience.

Take video games as an example. Open-world games allow players to explore freely, but they often provide guidance through tasks or visual cues to ensure that players have clear objectives and do not feel aimless (Gee, 2003). Similarly, educational apps may allow learners to choose topics they are interested in, yet still need to follow a structured curriculum to ensure learning progress (Anderson, 2016). Interactivity achieves the best results when users can both explore freely and be guided subtly, enhancing both engagement and effectiveness (Csikszentmihalyi, 1990).

Interactive experiences should also be immersive, enabling users to engage wholeheartedly while taking full advantage of the unique strengths of the medium they are in. For instance, touch-screen apps can utilize tactile feedback and smooth response design to enhance user interaction (Norman, 2002), while virtual reality can transport users into brand-new worlds through 360-degree immersive environments, making them feel as though they are physically present in a different space (Slater and Wilbur, 1997).

Immersion doesn’t merely mean dazzling visual effects or complicated technologies; it’s more about making users feel as if they were physically present and fully concentrated. For example, a mobile game might immerse users through sound effects, vibrations, and intuitive controls, creating an engaging environment that feels tangible and real (McMahan, 2003); or an art installation could create a memorable experience by using motion sensors, lights, and sounds, which actively involve the user in the space (Jones, 2018). Excellent interactive design should combine the unique capabilities of the medium and integrate them seamlessly into the experience, ensuring that users remain deeply engaged and fully immersed.

Interactive experiences should echo their surrounding environments – whether they are physical, cultural, or social environments. For example, an interactive installation located in a busy city square needs to consider factors such as noise, distractions, and differences in user participation levels. The experience must be adaptable to its environment to be effective and engaging for a diverse audience (Sutcliffe, 2015).

Relevance is also reflected in the alignment of emotions and themes. For instance, a mental health app should offer a calm, warm, and simple interface to evoke a sense of comfort and safety, while an interactive marketing campaign might attract users through engaging gamification elements. When an interactive experience is well-aligned with its environment and context, it feels more natural and meaningful, enhancing the overall user experience.

To sum up

Interactive experiences should empower users, allowing them to immerse themselves in the experience while resonating with their environment. It needs to strike a balance between freedom and structure, make full use of the characteristics of the medium, and be tailored to the specific scene. By following these principles, interactive experiences can go beyond the limitations of functionality and become a powerful tool for connection, creation, and participation.
