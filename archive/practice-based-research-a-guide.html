Week 1 Reading: Practice Based Research: A Guide

1 oct 20262026 年 10 月 1 日

这篇文章写于在学校的课程期间，原文是英文。

My second reading this week was Linda Candy’s Practice Based Research: A Guide (2006), written for students who make creative work as part of their research. After Schön’s idea that people learn by making and reflecting, I wanted to understand how that kind of learning can count as research.

Two kinds of practice research

Candy separates practice-related research into two types. If a creative artefact is the basis of the contribution to knowledge, the research is practice-based. If the research leads mainly to new understanding about practice itself, it is practice-led (Candy, 2006). The difference sounds small, but it changes what has to be shown. In practice-based research, the artefact is part of the argument, so a full understanding needs the work itself. In practice-led research, the findings can be explained in writing without the artefact.

What I take from it

Reading this, I realised that my blog sits between the two. My experiments produce artefacts, prototypes and tests that carry knowledge only when you see or use them. But my reflections are closer to practice-led writing, explaining what I learned about the way I work. I think a good research blog needs both: the artefact as evidence, and the text that says what the artefact taught me.

Candy also made me notice a gap. She explains why making can be research, but she says much less about how the making should be organised over time. If the artefact is the basis of the knowledge, then the process that produces it is not neutral. A process that leaves feedback until the end would also leave the learning until the end.

So this week I decided to look at that process directly, and compare development methods to choose one for my final project. Together with Schön’s reflection-in-action, this reading set up the two research questions I explore in the next posts: what people fail to notice in everyday situations, and how to develop a project so that making and reflecting happen in a cycle rather than in a straight line.
