The Bicentennial Man

这篇文章写于在学校的课程期间，原文是英文。

Overview

“Bicentennial Man” uses the story of the robot Andrew to explore in – depth the pursuit of humanity by artificial intelligence and the ethical and philosophical issues it raises.

The film centres around Andrew’s journey from an ordinary robot gradually seeking a human identity, challenging the boundaries between artificial intelligence and humanity. His self – discovery journey makes me reflect on what it truly means to be human: what exactly is “humanity”? Especially after experiencing physical and emotional transformations, can Andrew completely shed his robotic identity? Although his behaviours and emotions increasingly approximate those of humans, I believe he can never fully “become human” because his starting point and essence stem from programming and technology, not a natural life process. However, Andrew’s pursuit of a human identity shows the possibility of artificial intelligence constantly transcending its own limitations. At the same time, it raises an interesting question: if human characteristics can be replicated so precisely, does the definition of “human” need to be re – examined?

The film also focuses on the ethics of artificial intelligence and human rights. As Andrew gradually approaches humanity, he faces legal and ethical challenges. Especially after acquiring the ability to perceive, should he enjoy the same rights as humans? This deeply touches me, especially in today’s society where artificial intelligence is increasingly integrated into our lives. Should we grant AI equal rights to humans? Andrew’s desire to be recognised as human reflects profound thinking on whether artificial intelligence should have freedom, rights, and autonomy. It also makes me reflect on whether we should assume more ethical responsibilities when designing AI. Creating life forms that can perceive emotions and pain is not just a technical issue but also a moral one. When society confronts such beings, it needs to re – define the relationship between humans and machines and even re – examine our definition of the concept of “human”.

As a student studying creative computing, I feel that the film offers significant inspiration for the design of future intelligent systems. Andrew’s journey makes me realize that when developing highly autonomous systems, apart from pursuing technological advancement, we also need to consider the balance between ethics and human – centred design. In human – machine interaction, how to enable AI to embody human emotions without overly encroaching on human autonomy is a question worthy of in – depth consideration. At the same time, I also reflect on whether we might overlook the fundamental differences of AI due to the “human characteristics” it exhibits. This makes me more aware that the rapid development of technology must be premised on social consensus and an ethical framework to truly serve humanity rather than replace it.

Through Andrew’s experiences, Bicentennial Man deeply explores the ethical issues of artificial intelligence, human identity, and the creation of sentient life forms. This not only makes me reflect on how technology influences social roles but also prompts me to re – examine my responsibilities when designing interactive systems in the future. I believe that no matter how advanced technology becomes, we must always maintain respect for human nature. Only in this way can technology truly become a helpful force in human life rather than a threat.
